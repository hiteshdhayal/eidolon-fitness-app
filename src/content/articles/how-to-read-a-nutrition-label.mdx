The front of a packet is advertising. The back is information. Read the back.

## Start with the serving size

Everything on the label is for one serving, and the serving is often smaller than you think. A 30 g serving of chips is not the whole packet. Check how many servings are in the pack.

## Compare per 100 g

Use the per-100 g column to compare two products. Front-of-pack numbers can be per serving, which makes a small serving look better than it is.

## Protein, added sugar and sodium

- Protein: more protein per 100 g is a useful shortcut for snacks and staples.
- Added sugar: lower is better. Sugar in a whole fruit is not the same as added sugar.
- Sodium: multiply sodium in grams by 2.5 to get salt. Under 5 g of salt a day is the target.

## The FSSAI mark

Look for the FSSAI logo and the 14-digit licence number. A packaged food sold in India should carry it.

## What the label does not tell you

It will not tell you how the food fits your day. That is what the plan builder is for — it counts your whole week, not one packet.
